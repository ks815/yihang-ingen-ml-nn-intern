# Week 3 Recap

Week 3 extended the Psychologist Agent's fine-tune-plus-RAG-plus-safety methodology onto Fari's eldercare-companion dialogue, with Senpai as a secondary anchor for the RAG side. Three pieces: a QLoRA/DPO fine-tune of Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on 54 hand-curated ROUTINE/MILD_DISTRESS/ESCALATE preference pairs, a FAISS RAG pipeline over 13 eldercare-communication knowledge chunks (hit@1=0.846, hit@3=hit@5=0.923), and an independent safety/escalation checker evaluated on 18 labeled transcripts (83.3% accuracy, zero false negatives on ESCALATE).

The safety-check design is the part I kept comparing back to the Psychologist Agent's crisis-escalation logic, since it's the most directly transferable piece. Both use the same core idea: a check that sits outside the generative model rather than trusting the fine-tuned model to police itself, and both are tuned to be recall-first on the worst-case category — Psychologist Agent's 97% safety-detection figure and this week's 100% ESCALATE recall are the same design philosophy, just measured on different (and much smaller) test sets. The actual mechanism differs, though: Psychologist Agent's checker is more built into the trained model's behavior, while this week's is a standalone embedding-centroid classifier with a hard keyword override for unambiguous crisis phrases, kept deliberately separate from the DPO-tuned model so the safety layer doesn't inherit whatever failure modes the fine-tune has. That separation is also why I evaluated it on a labeled set that had nothing to do with the exemplar phrases or keywords used to build the checker — the program plan flags this exact circularity risk (checking a safety check against the same data used to design it), and it's a trap I only avoided because I wrote the exemplars before looking at the test transcripts, not because the architecture makes it impossible.

The one place this week is weaker than the Psychologist Agent original is honesty about scale: 54 training pairs and 18 safety-eval transcripts are small enough that a handful of flipped labels would move the headline numbers by several points, whereas Psychologist Agent's evaluation had more data behind its 97% figure. I'm treating this week's numbers as "the methodology works, direction is right" rather than something to quote as a stable production metric.

Practical lesson, separate from the ML: getting `trl`'s `DPOTrainer` running at all took real dependency archaeology — `transformers>=4.46` silently breaks `trl==0.9.6`'s trainer internals, torch 2.2.2 (the last macOS x86_64 build) caps `numpy`/`scipy` versions too, and even after all that, a dtype-casting gap inside trl 0.9.6 itself (`prepare_model_for_kbit_training` and `peft_module_casting_to_bf16` leaving norm layers and `lm_head` on different dtypes with no working autocast bridge) needed a manual workaround before the Colab T4 run would actually train. None of that shows up in a results table, but it's most of why this notebook needed real GPU debugging beyond what the Week 2 classifier ever required, and it's the reason the fine-tuning ablation could only be smoke-tested locally in `DRY_RUN` mode before the real run.

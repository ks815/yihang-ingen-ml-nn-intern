# Week 4 Final Recap

Looking back at the whole four weeks, this internship ended up covering a pretty wide range of things for such a short program. Week 1 was mostly reading and mapping: I went through InGen's five products (Fari, Senpai, Sentinel Prime AI, Aido Rover, Aido Humanoid) and tried to connect their PIC 2.0 architecture to the OpenClaw multi-agent system I built before. That part was more research than coding, but it was useful for figuring out which pieces of my past work would actually transfer and which ones (like real-time multi-robot coordination) I genuinely had no experience with yet.

Week 2 was my first time doing real deployment engineering instead of just training a model and stopping there. I built a synthetic sensor dataset for a classifier modeled after Aido Rover, trained a small feedforward network, and then quantized it to INT8 to see what would happen. I went in assuming quantization would obviously make things faster, and it didn't, it actually made the model slower on something this small, and I only found that out because I bothered to time it instead of assuming.

Week 3 was the biggest one. I fine-tuned a small open source language model with QLoRA and DPO, built a FAISS retrieval pipeline, and put together a safety checker for a companion robot dialogue task. This was also the week I got the most hands on with real infrastructure problems: my laptop has no GPU, so the actual training had to happen on Colab, and getting everything to run there took a lot of troubleshooting, dependency versions that didn't match up, a Colab session that reset itself and wiped my uploaded files, and a genuinely confusing bug where the model kept crashing because two parts of it ended up in different numeric precisions. None of that shows up in the final numbers, but it took real time and taught me a lot about how much of ML engineering is just making the environment behave.

Across all of it, the thing I'll take away most is how important it is to actually check your results instead of assuming they'll turn out the way you expect. Quantization didn't speed anything up. Comparing fine-tuning across a few configurations mattered more than I thought it would going in. Even a model that got fine-tuned still missed a warning sign it should have caught, which was a good reminder that you can't just trust a single model to catch everything on its own. I feel like I came out of this internship with a much more concrete sense of what it actually takes to bring an ML idea from an experiment to something that could reasonably be deployed.
